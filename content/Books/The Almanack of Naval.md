I found this book at a time I really needed inspiration. I'm going to be a father in six short months and I've felt static in my career trajectory. Having ambition with no real signals of progress is a torture in it's own right. His opinions are a lot of the same opinions I hold. Down to what he calls rational Buddhism. I've only learned the very beginnings of Buddhism, but the Buddhist views on the right way to live a life resonate with me up until the mythology of the religion)

My take away from this book is that all of my assumptions have been right about success. What I've been lacking is the self-confidence to follow my intuition. To believe that I'm capable of being the best at something. Which stops from from even finding what my true calling is ([[How to do great work]]). What's next for me to be myself unapologetically, which I've know is my next step for a long time, but seeing someone "successful" having it written down is reassuring. We all need a little bit of reassurance. But my next hurdle is my path to greatness is being comfortable with the idea that to be great, I may need to be seen at some point. To be able to put myself out there and put my name on something I've built, learned, or believe. Open. myself up to criticism, which is something I've feared deep down while my rational mind knows it's a pointless fear that leads to nothing, leads to a life of nothing.

In a funny way, knowing a life that will depend on me gives me the courage to leave that fear behind. I suppose that's what all new fathers talk about. That shift in perspective. Caring about what people I don't even really care about think about me holds me back from being an interesting person in my own right. Placating to others makes me a boring mixed reflection of the people around me. Fundamentally I know that's wrong and I'm embarrassed to think of a version of time where my child is born into a world and sees their father that way. That's what I won't let happen. 

In my core I know I'm too smart, capable, and ambitious to be a never ran. Even running will set me up to provide security to my family and to myself. There's a life I live where I'm free. And that's what's important to me. That myself and my family and healthy and free. Before my time ends on this earth I want to have tried. When my life ends nothing really matters I suppose, but if I get to choose what kind of existence I lead, it would be one where I was true to myself and did all that I could to make sure my loved ones were cared for.
